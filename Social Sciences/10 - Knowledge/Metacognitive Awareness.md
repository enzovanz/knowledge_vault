adwwdasa## Summary

**Metacognitive awareness** is your awareness and understanding of **your own thinking and learning processes**—essentially, _knowing how you think_.

It includes three main kinds of awareness:

- **What you know:** recognizing your strengths, weaknesses, and gaps in knowledge. _“I understand the concept, but I couldn’t explain why it works.”_
- **How you learn:** knowing which strategies work for you. _“I retain this better when I solve problems rather than reread notes.”_
- **When and why to use strategies:** recognizing when you need to change your approach. _“I’m reading this paragraph repeatedly without understanding it; I should slow down and break it apart.”_

A related concept is **metacognitive regulation**: awareness is _recognizing_ what’s happening in your thinking; regulation is _doing something about it_—planning, monitoring your understanding, changing strategies, and evaluating the result.

In short: **cognition = thinking; metacognition = thinking about and managing your thinking.**

## Explanation

Metacognitive awareness helps solve the problem of confusing familiarity with understanding. You can read a sentence, recognize the words, and still not be able to use the idea. Metacognition is the internal check that notices the difference.

It works by turning attention back onto the learning process itself:

- Am I actually understanding this, or just recognizing it?
- Could I explain it without looking?
- What part is still vague?
- What strategy should I use next: reread, summarize, draw, solve a problem, ask for an example?

This is especially useful when studying abstract subjects. It helps you detect weak understanding early, choose better learning strategies, and avoid the false confidence that comes from passive review.

## Example

While reading about [[Own-price elasticity of demand]], you might feel that the definition makes sense. A metacognitive check would ask: "Can I predict what happens to revenue when demand is elastic versus inelastic?"

If you cannot answer, the issue is not effort but diagnosis. You have identified the gap, so the next step might be to create examples, calculate a few scenarios, or explain the idea in your own words.
