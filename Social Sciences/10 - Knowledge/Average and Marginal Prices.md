## Summary

The **average price** spreads total spending across all units consumed, while the **marginal price** is what must be paid for one more unit. The marginal price usually has the stronger effect on whether someone takes the next drink, trip, or other action.

## Explanation

An upfront fee and a per-use price create different incentives even when they collect the same amount of money. Once an upfront fee has been paid, it does not change the cost of consuming one more unit. The fee may affect whether someone participates at all, but a marginal price affects how much a participant consumes.

This distinction matters when the purpose of a charge is to discourage each additional action. An annual vehicle fee may influence whether someone owns a car, but it makes the next trip feel free. A per-trip charge gives the driver a reason to reconsider that specific journey.

Average and marginal prices answer different questions:

- **Average price:** total amount paid divided by the number of units consumed.
- **Marginal price:** the additional amount paid for the next unit.

## Example

A student pays £10 to enter a party with unlimited drinks and consumes 20 drinks. The average price is £0.50 per drink, but the marginal price of the next drink is zero. Raising admission to £20 may discourage some students from attending, yet those who attend still face a marginal drink price of zero. Charging per drink would address their incentive to consume another one directly.
